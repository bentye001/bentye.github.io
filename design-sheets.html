Sheet 01

Design, tolerancing and stress analysis · 2025/26

Knuckle joint

A pin joint in three parts: fork, single eye and pin. The sections were calculated from the standard design equations for a knuckle joint before anything was modelled: the pin diameter from double shear, the fork and eye thickness from bearing stress, and the rods from direct tension at the design load, each against an allowable derived from the yield strength. The assembly was then built to those dimensions and accurately geometrically dimensioned and toleranced across all four sheets.

Parts
Fork, single eye, pin
Material
Steel, 415 MPa yield
Sizing
Standard design equations
Drawings
4 sheets, balloons and parts list
Analysis
Static stress, parabolic mesh
Load case
2 × 35 kN opposing axial

What the analysis showed

  • The static stress study was the check on those calculations: opposing 35 kN axial forces against a fixed constraint, a parabolic mesh refined adaptively on von Mises stress.
  • Peak stress is 149 MPa and lands in the rod sections rather than at the pin or the eye, so the joint itself is not the weak point. Minimum safety factor 2.79 against a 415 MPa yield, with total displacement peaking at 0.068 mm.
  • The tolerancing is what I would change. A ±0.25 sits on nearly every dimension; in production that becomes a general tolerance note with explicit callouts only where function demands them, and the fit between pin and bore needs specifying properly.

Sheet 02

Final year project · full product cycle

Instrumented insole platform

A 3D printed insole measuring plantar pressure from eight sensors embedded inside the structure. The plantar surface came from a LiDAR scan of my own foot, cleaned up in Blender and used as a cutting body in Fusion 360, so the platform conforms to the foot it was made for. Sensors sit in printed cavities rather than being trapped by a paused print, which keeps them located, free of pre-load and replaceable.

What it showed

  • A force sensitive resistor responds to the structure above it as much as to the load, so I designed a jig to apply controlled vertical loading to test cubes printed with different contact geometries and infill densities, in 2.5 kg steps to 12.5 kg.
  • The dome geometry gave the best balance of activation threshold and sensitivity, and went into the full build.
  • The calibration did not transfer to the assembled insole, so the final analysis is a normalised comparison rather than an absolute pressure. Fixing that is the first thing I would do next.

Sheet 03

Pair project · enclosure and sensing

Instrumented pillbox

A medication adherence proof of concept, built with one other student. The assembly packages a Raspberry Pi 3, HQ camera, load cell and user feedback hardware into printed parts: box, lid, base, mount and camera enclosure. A latch designed in Fusion 360 trips a push button as the lid closes, so the measurement sequence is event driven rather than polling continuously.

What I did

  • The mechanical packaging and the sensing chain: the load cell mount, calibration through an HX711 amplifier, and 3D printed replica pills weighed to set a per pill threshold that separates a real removal from load cell drift.
  • Trained the detector on a 2,000 image dataset and exported it to NCNN so inference runs on the Pi rather than in the cloud, reaching 90% precision and 83.6% recall.
  • The system can confirm that medication left the box. It cannot confirm anyone swallowed it, and we said so.
